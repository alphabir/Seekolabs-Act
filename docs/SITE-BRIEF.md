## 0. The fork everything else hangs off

> **DECIDE.** The original brief sells *Client Solutions — turnkey app development* on the
> same page as the existing hero ribbon, which says **"Publishing House Model — Not an
> Agency."** A visitor reads both in one scroll and concludes one of them is marketing.

Two coherent answers. Pick one.

**A. Publisher first, partner work by exception.** *(recommended)*
You build and publish your own apps. You take on a small number of outside products where
you would have built something similar anyway. Client work is framed as selective, not as
a service menu. This keeps the existing positioning intact, and scarcity reads as strength.

**B. Studio.** You build for clients, and your own apps are the portfolio that proves it.
Honest, common, and immediately competitive with every other agency on price.

The rest of this document is written for **A**. Say the word and I will rewrite for B.

## 1. Title and tagline

**Title (unchanged, it is already right for SEO):**
`SeekoLabs | Digital App Publishing House & Product Studio`

**Hero tagline:** *Building and scaling apps designed for growth.*

> **Changed.** You liked *"Vibecoding the future of mobile experiences."* It has energy and
> it is current. It also undercuts the exact reader `growth@` exists to catch: someone
> paying you to ship to your developer account is buying carefulness, and "vibecoding"
> signals the opposite to that reader. It also fights your own headline, *We Engineer &
> Publish*, and the game's own *Engineered Properly*.
>
> **So keep it, but move it.** It is a great line about *how the team works* and a risky
> one about *what clients buy*. Put it in "Who We Are" (section 4) where it describes
> culture, and it costs you nothing.

**One-liner under the tagline:**

> SeekoLabs is an independent app publishing house in Kolkata. We engineer, launch and
> scale our own software, and we take on a small number of partner products each year.

**Primary CTA:** `Start a project` → `growth@seekolabs.tech`
**Secondary CTA:** `See our work` → jumps to Our Products

## 2. What we do

Three offerings. The third one is rewritten.

**App Publishing**
Original games and utilities, built in-house and launched on our own developer account.
We own the roadmap, the release cadence and the support queue.

**Partner Products**
A small number of outside products each year, taken end to end: requirements, build, Play

ours, whichever fits.

**Growth & Monetization**

> **Rewritten.** The original said *"Implementing seamless in-app subscription
> architectures and optimized ad stacks to maximize user lifetime value."* Two problems.
> It claims expertise the portfolio does not yet evidence, and it sits directly above a

> We monetize a product when the product has earned it, not before. Ludo Apex ships free,
> with no advertising and no in-app purchases, because that is what makes it good. When a

That version sells the same capability and is not contradicted by the thing directly below it.

### Spotlight: Ludo Apex

Split layout. Device mockup on one side, copy on the other. The mockup needs real
screenshots from the current build — see section 6.

**Name:** Ludo Apex
**Hook:** The classic board game, re-engineered for the modern player.

**Feature bullets — the corrected set:**

- **No ads. Not fewer ads, none.** No interstitials, no rewarded video, no banner.
- **No sign-up and no account.** Nothing to create, nothing to leak.
- **Plays fully offline.** No connection needed at any point.
- **Zero data collected.** No analytics SDK, no advertising ID, no telemetry.
- **Three tiers of AI** built from real Ludo strategy, not random legal moves.
- **Two to four players on one device**, taking turns around a shared board.

> **These replace the originals, which described an app that does not exist.**
> *"Advanced matchmaking & custom house rules"* and *"Built-in voice chat for private
> lobbies"* need accounts, a server and network code. Ludo Apex has none of those. Beyond
> being untrue, Google cross-checks marketing copy against your listing and your build, and
> your own submission guide already avoids the words "online" and "multiplayer" for this
> reason. Shipping those bullets risks the listing.
>
> The honest set is also the stronger pitch, because almost no free Ludo app can claim it.

**CTA, pre-launch:** `Coming soon to Google Play` (not a link until the listing is live)
**CTA, at launch:** the official Google Play badge → the store URL

Never ship a live-looking button that goes nowhere. The first cohort bounces and does not
come back.

## 5. Contact and email routing

The routing strategy in the original brief is sound. Two addresses, two audiences.

| Address | Used for | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| `hello@seekolabs.tech` | Support, bug reports, user feedback | Footer, app store listings, privacy policy |
| `growth@seekolabs.tech` | Client work, partnerships | Every B2B CTA button |

> **One warning, and it has a deadline.** The site currently uses `connect@` and
> `support@`. `support@seekolabs.tech` is already written into the Ludo Apex privacy policy
> and is the developer contact that will go on the Play listing. Google publishes that
> address and requires it to work.
>
> **Decide the addresses before you submit to Play, then never change them.** Changing a
> developer contact after submission means editing the listing and the hosted policy
> together, and a mismatch between them is a rejection reason.
>
> **DECIDE:** switch to `hello@` everywhere now, or keep `support@` for the app and use
> `hello@` only on the website. Either works. Drifting between them does not.

### Assets you have to produce

6. **A 1200×630 share image.** The current one is a 256×256 icon, which is why the page

### Bugs to fix first

7. **The contact form is broken in production.** It POSTs to `/api/contact`, and on Vercel
   that returns 404, because Vercel serves `dist/` as static files and never runs
   `server.ts`. `/api/health` 404s live too. Any "Join the Beta Waitlist" form has the same
   problem. Three ways out: a Vercel serverless function under `api/`, a hosted form
   service, or plain `mailto:` links. **DECIDE.**
8. **`/server.cjs` and `/server.cjs.map` are publicly downloadable** from the live site. The
   build writes the Express bundle into `dist/`, so Vercel serves it, and the source map
   hands out the full `server.ts` source. No credentials are in it, so this is low
   severity, but it should not be public. Drop `--sourcemap` and point `--outfile` outside
   `dist/`.
9. **The footer's Privacy Policy and Terms links scroll to the contact form.** There is a
   `TODO` in `Footer.tsx` saying exactly this. Both pages need to exist.

### Build work, ready when you are

    system as the Ludo Apex pages, wired into the footer.
11. **The React sections rewritten** to this brief once section 0 is settled.
